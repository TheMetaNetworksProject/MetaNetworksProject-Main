## Background

The taxon you will work through for your first sheet is the **Graceful Black-throated Trogon** (*Trogon tenellus*)!!

![Graceful Black-throated Trogon](https://static.avicommons.org/bkttro7-416410115-900.jpg)

*Graceful Black-throated Trogon. Credit: Adam Jackson, Avicommons.*

Trogons are a tropical family of birds that can be find in the Americas, Africa and southern Asia. The name trogon comes from a Greek word meaning "nibbling," which references the fact that these birds make their nests by gnawing holes into trees. Trogons are the only birds (and only animals in general!) with a heterodactyl toe arrangement, meaning their 3rd and 4th digits point forward, while their 1st and 2nd digits point backwards. 

![bird foot arrangement](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/bb/Bird_feet_-_toe_arrangement_%28dactyly%29_EN.gif)

*Bird toe arrangements. Credit: Darekk2, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons.*

Trogons are reclusive birds, and thus relatively little is known about their biology, despite being diversely colorful and popular among birdwatchers! 

The Graceful Black-throated Trogon's taxonomic history highlights this. It was first described as a unique species in the 1860s, but was long treated as a subspecies of the Black-throated Trogon (*T. rufus*). In 2022, *T. rufus* was split into 4 species by the American Ornithological Society, one of which being *T. tenellus*. Because of this recent split, there are relatively few papers that use the name *T. tenellus*.
